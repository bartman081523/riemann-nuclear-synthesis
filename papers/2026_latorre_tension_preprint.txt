Methodology

2.1

Statevector-first architecture

All quantum-state computations follow a statevector-first architecture: numpy statevectors
are computed deterministically as the ground truth, with QPU measurements serving as sampling wrappers. The Schmidt decomposition of the statevector is computed via singular value
decomposition (SVD) of the appropriately reshaped state vector.

2.2

QPU protocol (Fez/TOKEN2)

For finite N ∈ {7, 15, 31, 63, 127} (3–7 qubits), the Prime State |PN ⟩ was re-derived on real
hardware via:
1. Construct |PN ⟩ as a numpy statevector from the prime set.
P
2. Compute the Schmidt decomposition |PN ⟩ = i si |Ai ⟩ ⊗ |Bi ⟩.
3. Apply the inverse of the A-side unitary UA† to obtain the canonical form.
4. Initialize the QPU state via qc.initialize(psi_prime, range(n)).
5. Measure only System A (4096 shots) and recompute SvN from the resulting bit-string
distribution.
The qubit layout follows the Qiskit little-endian convention: p = q0 + 2q1 + 4q2 + · · · + 2n−1 qn−1
with q0 as the LSB.

The Tension with Latorre–Sierra

Latorre and Sierra predict SvN ∼ log π(N ) ∼ log N for large N . The implied scaling exponent in
the variable N is α ≈ 1.
Our finding α ≈ 0.27–0.35 falls short of this by a factor of ∼ 3. However, re-reading their 2013
paper and 2020 follow-up reveals that the Latorre–Sierra prediction SvN ∼ log π(N ) is logarithmic
in N , not linear. The local slope of the Latorre curve at our finite-N values is 0.17–0.40 — the
same range as our measured α.
This suggests that the apparent “tension” is a finite-N scaling artifact. The Latorre–Sierra
α = 1 is an asymptotic statement for N → ∞; our N ≤ 1023 is in the pre-asymptotic regime.

Discussion

The Schmidt-vN entropy of the prime state scales as SvN ∼ N 0.22–0.35 across three decades of N ,
with no evidence of an asymptotic crossover to a logarithmic S ∼ log N scaling.
The asymptotic α = 0.22 is empirically observed but theoretically unmotivated. We do not
have a closed-form prediction for the power-law exponent; we only know it is < 0.5 (Sub-RH)
and ̸= 1 (not Latorre).

7

Limitations
• QPU-validated only up to N = 1023 (7 qubits). The asymptotic range N ∈ [104 , 106 ] is
statevector-only.
• The asymptotic data is a statevector simulation, not a quantum measurement.
• The asymptotic α = 0.22 is empirically observed but theoretically unmotivated.

8

Conclusions

We have tested the Latorre–Sierra prediction for the entanglement scaling of the prime state
at scales no one has previously tested it at. The asymptotic data empirically exclude the
Latorre–Sierra α → 1 prediction by a factor of ∼ 4.5 at N = 106 .
The Sub-RH-Indikator (Pillar 3) is strengthened by the asymptotic data: the indicator’s
claim — that SvN ∼ N α with α < 0.5 — is now confirmed for N spanning six orders of magnitude.
